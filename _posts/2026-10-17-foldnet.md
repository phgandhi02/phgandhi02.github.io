This post is a review of the FoldNet and FoldNet++ papers.

## What are they doing?

They develop an extensive synthetic dataset for T-shirt folding and unfolding
(ie getting clothes into a flattened stated from an arbitrarily crumbled state).
They generate T-shirt meshes with semantic keypoint annotations and then use a
rule-based manipulation framework (ie heuristic) to fold and unfold clothes from
abitrary configurations. They evaluate various model types like vision-action
models (e.g. visuomotor policy), vision-action-language models (VLAs), and
world-action models (WAMs), in both sim and real-world evaluation environments.
They achieve over 90% end-to-end task success rates on arbitrary initial garment
states.

They note that they are able to get better performance compared to alternative
techniques like action primities (a traditional technique), model-based
techniques which try to model garment dynamics to plan actions, and end-to-end
manipulation policies using behavior cloning which has become a more popular
technique in recent years.

## Why do I think this is useful?

They outline a strategy for training a policy without the need for expensive
human demonstrations common in behavior cloning approaches. By using the their
pipeline for simulated data, it's possible to create a complete end-to-end model
training pipeline by running a simulation, getting hundreds of thousands of
samples with the option to run domain randomization, and then training a model
of choice to obtain a manipulation policy.

## How does this contribute to my research?

This contributes to my research by providing a potential data source and
methodology for training a model to autonomously fold and unfold clothes. I
would need to obtain their trained weights and then see if I can improve the 90%
task success rate by adjusting the model or seeing how it performs on other
types of clothes or configurations. I could combine it with other publicly
available datasets to make a better trained model.
